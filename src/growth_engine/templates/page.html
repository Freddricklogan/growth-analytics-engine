1. Which channels earn the enrolments

Four attribution models on the same journeys. Heuristics split credit by position; the Markov model credits each channel by how much conversion falls when it is removed from the transition graph. The generator's true per-channel effects are known, so the last row reports how well each model's ranking agrees with them. Attribution is accounting for credit, not a measurement of cause; section 3 is the only causal estimate on this page.

4. What an enrolment costs and returns

Acquisition cost from spend over Markov-attributed enrolments; lifetime value from the cohort curve at month $horizon, using only cohorts old enough to have observed it.

$econ_table

Method, stated

  • Markov attribution: first-order transition matrix over (start), channels, (conversion), (null); removal effect = 1 − P(conversion | channel redirected to null) ÷ P(conversion); credit proportional to removal effect and summing to observed conversions.
  • Retention counts a user active in month k after acquisition if they made at least one purchase in that month; LTV is cumulative revenue per acquired user, averaged across cohorts weighted by size.
  • Uplift: difference in conversion rate, treated minus control, with a Wald 95% interval; segments are pre-registered, not searched.
  • CAC = channel spend ÷ Markov-attributed conversions. Payback is the first month the cumulative revenue curve reaches CAC; “not within window” is reported rather than extrapolated.
